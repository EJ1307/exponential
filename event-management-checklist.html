Event Management Checklist: Pre-Event, Event Day and Post-Event

A complete event management checklist covering pre-event planning, event day operations, and post-event follow up, built from real corporate event execution.

← Back to Blog

Quick answer

A complete event management checklist has three phases. Pre-event covers objective, budget, venue, vendors, branding, and registration. Event day covers setup, registration desk, protocol, technical control, and hospitality. Post-event covers teardown, vendor settlement, content delivery, and a written report.

Phase one: pre-event

This is where eighty percent of the outcome is decided. Work through these items in order and sign off each one before moving on.

  • Define the objective, delegate count, and success measures
  • Approve the budget with a contingency line
  • Book the date, venue, and room block
  • Build the agenda and confirm speakers in writing
  • Appoint sound, light, LED, catering, and print vendors
  • Approve branding, signage, and stage design
  • Open registration and start tracking confirmations
  • Plan travel, airport pickups, and hotel allocation
  • Prepare the run sheet and share it with every vendor

Phase two: event day

Event day is execution, not decision making. If a decision is being taken on the morning of the event, something was missed earlier.

  • Complete setup and technical checks before delegates arrive
  • Open the registration desk at least sixty minutes early
  • Brief the protocol team on VIP arrival and seating
  • Keep a control point with the run sheet, microphones, and spares
  • Manage stage transitions with a dedicated backstage coordinator
  • Monitor food service timings against session breaks
  • Capture photography and video against a shot list
  • Keep a written log of anything that goes wrong

Phase three: post-event

The last phase is the one most teams skip, and it is the one that compounds value across future events.

  • Supervise teardown and confirm venue handover
  • Collect and file all vendor invoices
  • Reconcile actual spend against budget
  • Share thank you notes and speaker certificates
  • Deliver the photo and video gallery to stakeholders
  • Collect delegate feedback while it is fresh
  • Write a short report with attendance, spend, and lessons

Use one master document

Checklists fail when they live in five different files. Keep one master sheet with owners and deadlines against every line, and make it the only version anyone works from.

Assign a single name to each task. Tasks owned by a team are tasks owned by nobody.

Frequently asked questions

What should be on an event day checklist?

Setup completion, technical checks, registration desk readiness, VIP protocol briefing, stage and session transitions, food service timings, media capture, and a written incident log.

When should the registration desk open?

At least sixty minutes before the first session. For events above three hundred delegates, ninety minutes is safer, with separate counters for pre-registered guests and walk ins.

What is usually forgotten in event planning?

Buffer time, contingency budget, and post-event follow up. Teams plan the event itself in detail and then leave no room for delays and no plan for what happens after.

Who owns the checklist on event day?

One event manager should own the master run sheet. Vendors and coordinators report against it, but only one person updates it, so there is never a conflicting version.
